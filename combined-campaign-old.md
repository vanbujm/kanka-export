# Corruption

Corruption represents the effects of interacting with Unrefined, or Poorly Refined Yggdrasil's Sap. Each PC has 4 "charges" as represented by their [Yggdrasil's Sap Corruption item](https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/9836820-yggdrasils-sap-corruption) on DND Beyond. Long rests clear 1 charge per rest. 

Additionally, any time a creature takes a long rest with Corruption, they must make a Wisdom Saving Throw in order to complete the Long Rest successfully. The DC of this save depends on the level of Corruption accrued at the beginning of the rest, starting with 1 level = DC 3\. Each additional level increases the DC by 3\. On a failed save, the character does not remove Corruption at the end of the rest, although all other benefits are still gained. Finally, upon failing the save .

Anytime someone interacts with Unrefined/Poorly Refined Sap, or uses Sap in some dangerous way, they must make a DC 18 saving throw (generally Constitution) to avoid taking Corruption. Some gear and abilities allow you to "soak" charges. When a charge is "soaked," it leaves no damage. If you must take multiple charges, you can soak the amount the gear or ability allows and take the rest.

While currently their are no mechanical drawbacks to having Corruption while awake, it is encouraged to roleplay your character's Corruption. You could choose a mania or phobia from Call of Cthulhu's sample lists, or a Fallout from Heart (see below), or have some physical representation of the Corruption, or some other effect that you deem fun and fitting.

If any creature accrues 4 points of Corruption they: 

* Become [Incapacitated](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#IncapacitatedCondition) for at least 10 minutes  
   * A **_Greater Restoration_** spell cast on you ends this effect.
* Clear the "charges"  
   * Any overflowing marks are carried over.
* Take a Scar

If any character accumulates 8 or more Corruption as the result of a single event, they lose control of their character permanently.

### Levels of Corruption

---

**_Light Exposure:_**

* Corruption: 0 + 1d2
* _Handling Unrefined Sap Briefly:_ A sapper or researcher accidentally touches raw sap with bare skin for a few seconds before pulling away.
* _Exposure to Contaminated Air or Water:_ Drinking water that has trace amounts of Sap corruption or working where Sap dust is present in the air.
* _Using a Poorly Made Device:_ A mage channels power through a cracked Sap crystal that isn’t properly refined, causing minor corruption over repeated use.
* _Interacting with an Infected Creature:_ Coming into contact with the fluids of a low-level, corrupted creature (such as its saliva, sweat, or minor wounds).

**_Medium Exposure:_**

* Corruption: 2 + 1d2
* _Wearing Corrupted Gear:_ Using a weapon or armor infused with sap that has not been properly stabilized. The corruption slowly seeps into the wielder.
* _Frequent Exposure to Unrefined Sap:_ Sappers or researchers working near raw deposits daily without proper precautions.
* _Significant Physical Contact:_ A person is splashed with the blood of a heavily corrupted creature, such as a sap-mutated beast or a humanoid in the later stages of corruption.
* _Using a Large Amount of Poorly Refined Sap in Magic:_ A caster channels a high-level spell through unstable sap, overloading their body with its magic.
* _Consuming Sap:_ A user smokes, eats, or drinks Sap

**_Heavy Exposure:_**

* Corruption: 4 + 1d4 (or more)
* _Direct Contact with a Large Sap Deposit:_ Falling into or being submerged in a pool of unrefined Sap.
* _Wielding an Extremely Corrupt Artifact:_ Attempting to use a legendary, but dangerously unstable, Sap-powered weapon or device.
* _Severe Contamination from a Creature:_ Being bitten, clawed, or injected with fluids from a highly corrupted being, like a Burster.
* _Long-Term Stay in a High-Corruption Zone:_ Spending days or weeks in an area where Sap corruption has fully overtaken the environment.
* _Experimental Infusions:_ Undergoing forced experimentation with raw Sap infusion in an attempt to gain power.

### Examples

---

_Alex is exploring the Sap Mines in simple street clothes, they have recently taken 2 Corruption and have never interacted with Sap before. Some horrible creature drops from the ceiling and bursts into a shower of goo. Alex needs to make a Con Save to avoid the Corruption.They only roll a 6; a fail. This is considered "Heavy Exposure" and they take 6 marks of Corruption. This brings their total to 8\. Unfortunately, Alex is no longer Alex._

_Casey is experimenting with a new Sap Infused Wand, they have accumulated 3 Corruption, but they have done this a few times through their life. Unfortunately, something goes wrong with their experiment and energy erupts from the wand. Casey needs to make an Int Save to avoid the Corruption. Poor Casey rolls an 8 total. This could be considered "Light Exposure" and they take 2 marks of Corruption. This brings their total to 5, they are immediately Incapacitated for 10 minutes, clear their charges, carry over the 1, then take a Scar._

# Scar

Scars are permanent changes that occur to characters after accumulating too much Corruption, both mechanically and narratively. You and the DM work together to determine a scar that best reflects the lasting effect of the damage and note it somewhere. Then, shift one proficiency you have in any skill to another to reflect how the scar changes the way your character interacts with the world.

### Example Scars

| **Event**              | **Scar**          | **Result**                                                                     |
| ---------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Creature spits in face | Weakened Eyesight | Can't see well, and quick to lash out: _\-Perception, +Intimidation_           |
| Used Corrupted Armor   | Chitinous Growths | Small, hardened plates form on body: _\-Acrobatics, +Athletics_                |
| Drank Tainted Water    | Veins of Light    | Veins glow, giving off an alluring otherworldly aura: _\-Stealth, +Persuasion_ |
| Fell in a Vat of Sap   | Reality Altered   | Natural world makes no sense, but magic does: _\-Nature, +Arcana_              |
| Held a lump of Sap     | Warped Digits     | Off putting to look at, but dextrous: _\-Performance, +Sleight of Hand_        |
| Smoked Sap             | Cannot Sleep      | Constantly vigilant, but a bit crazy: _\-Insight, +Perception_                 |

It is also possible to have other modifications, but in all cases, there should be something given, something taken away, and a critical narrative change should also occur.

# Doyle

is_dead: false
races: Human

Doyle didn’t mean to end up on Eclipsion. He was simply doing what any dutiful 17-year-old might: delivering a mysterious lockbox to the family vault—a task made considerably harder by needing to take an urgent detour to the outhouse en route. Opening that rickety door, he was sucked into a portal (as one does) and spat out in a land where even the chickens have teeth. 

Armed with nothing but a mild panic and the aforementioned lockbox—which, as it turns out, contained his master's old spellbook—Doyle clawed his way through those first few years. Eventually, his resourcefulness and magical sort-of-competence earned him enough to open the Endless Legs Emporium in the Undercity, a shop specializing in “previously enchanted, gently cursed, and affordably mediocre” magical items. He’s not exactly thriving, but he has food (sometimes identifiable), shelter (mostly watertight), and a reputation for being the only merchant willing to haggle with demons over the cost of a slightly used Wand of Almost-Fireball.

This reputation has led to some encounters with some rather curious clientele. Some Githyanki, some peculiar individuals in drenched cloaks, several visits from Ebon Syndicate representatives, and one somewhat regular patron: Professor Torvil Wesa. Wesa's knowledge and advice has been extremely helpful to Doyle as he develops his own skills.

Doyle doesn’t want to be here. Home may have been mundane, but it didn’t involve all of this weirdness. It has been 20 years now and he has had enough. The problem is, Doyle has no idea where "here" is, let alone how to get "there." Every map he’s studied fails to list a land named "Blightlyshire-on-the-Wolds" (though he suspects the locals might just spell it funny). Worse, no wizard, sage, or tavern drunk has even heard of his world. For all he knows, he might be stuck in the back-end of some cosmic experiment where the only way out involves saying the right obscure incantation while standing on one leg at sunset during a leap year.

Most of his days are split between his shop, either of the libraries, Astralis Athenaeum or Echoes Nook (where he constantly annoys the librarians by mispronouncing arcane words), and the Broken Drum Tavern. His favorite shop would be the goulash shop down the road, ominously named Food. Despite being made from a jelly-like substance of dubious origin, the goulash tastes remarkably like the venison stew of Doyle’s childhood. It’s not perfect, but it’s the closest he’s found to home-cooked comfort—and it’s much cheaper than therapy.

### Items

Doyle possesses the robes and spellbook of his former master as well as an enlightened version of Find Familiar that summons a Chest of Endless Legs.

# Nim

is_dead: false
races: Locathah
families: Nolum's brood

Nim is from a small community of Locathah found deep in an underwater sinkhole in the Shallow Sea. This community called themselves The Istishians.  
  
Nim was born with dreams that no one could explain. While most Istishians only glimpse visions after decades spent meditating near the vents, Nim began dreaming young—first of deep trenches and motionless currents, then of impossible things: drifting lights in a black sea above, ships that swam through stars, and a presence that watched but never spoke. In one dream, he stood before a massive, veiled figure in a reef of obsidian, and when a single mote of starlight touched his brow, he awoke gasping—somehow on the surface, saltwater flooding his lungs.

His people whispered that he was _Tide-broken_, cast out by fate or called by something they dare not name. Some think it’s the The Silent One reaching beyond the sea. Others fear it might be something far older—or worse, something new. Nim doesn’t know for certain what calls to him, only that he _feels_ its pull, stronger with every starlit sky. Whatever the truth, he believes his dreams are leading him toward something waiting in the stars—somewhere the silence isn't empty, but alive.

He came up to the city about a year or two ago and has been trying to get passage on a ship to the stars. His dreams draw him there and he wants to discover his destiny. He keeps the existence of his community as his closest guarded secret. He has seen the Sap mines and how people are treated up here. If the "surface dwellers" ever found out about the vast sumps of Sap found deep below the surface. His whole family could be wiped out.

One night, early into his foray on the surface, Nim was ambushed by a group of Scurry lurking in the shadows. During the fight, he was holding his own, however their claws left deep gashes grievously wounding Nim. Just as he was about to collapse, a professor from the Celestial Conclave University happened upon the scene. Using intricate illusions, the professor drove the creatures away and offered to tend to Nim’s wounds. Suspicious of surface-dwellers and certain there would be a demand for gold or a favor in return, Nim refused the offer and fled into the night, nursing his injuries alone.

Seeing some signs of hope, he works for the Ebon Syndicate, with Prithtic Zofrinn acting as his "handler". They keep stringing him along with promises of getting him on a ship. But really they only want to use him as a hitman. He's gained "local legend" status amongst the Undercity as "The Drowned Man" a bogeyman clad in black cloth who only leaves a pool of brine (and blood, lots of blood) at the scene of the crime. In reality, Nim hates "killing savages" and is growing more desperate for an escape by the day.  

Despite the unusual nature of their friendship, Nim and Myrddin Manaphir have grown close—two curious souls drawn together by shared dreams and quiet determination. They often meet at The Brackish Clam, a cozy, sea-worn tavern tucked into the lower reaches of Eclipsion, where the air smells faintly of salt and the lanternlight dances like waves. Over warm drinks and half-sketched plans, they talk of spelljammers and distant stars, trading stories of the worlds they’d visit if they could ever get their hands on a ship.

Myrddin’s Sap-infused arm needs regular infusions of Nim’s blood to stay functional, and Nim is more than happy to help—seeing it as a small price to pay for the companionship and sense of purpose their bond brings. Between the laughter, quiet moments, and star-filled daydreams, the two have become co-conspirators in a shared hope: that someday soon, they’ll leave the surface behind and sail the stars, free to chart their own course.  
  
Nim has a quiet fondness for Umpledimps’ Antiques & Oddities, a cluttered little shop filled with strange trinkets and forgotten wonders. He likes the way it smells—salt, dust, and just a hint of magic—and how the shelves seem to rearrange themselves when no one’s watching. Frynn Umpledimp's wild enthusiasm and Lollie Umpledimp's careful order remind Nim of something warm and distant, like the rhythm of home currents. Though he rarely buys anything, he visits often, listening to Frynn’s rambling stories or letting Lollie gently scold him for tracking in seawater. They don’t say it outright, but there's an ease between them that feels almost familial, and Nim, though he’d never admit it, doesn’t mind one bit.

# Astral Sea

The Astral Sea, like Wildspace, is a void; however, it is not an airless one. Here, you can breathe normally and exist indefinitely, never aging and never needing food or drink. You can propel yourself through the Astral Sea with the power of your mind alone, though many astral voyagers wisely travel in well-armed ships, for this place is the home of a host of fearsome creatures. Here travelers might find the petrified hulks of dead gods and swirling pools of color that serve as portals to other planes of existence.

Time in the Astral flowed at the same rate on a Prime Material plane but the effects of time were slowed almost to a stop―a thousand years in the Astral plane felt like only a day to the traveler. Hence, it was sometimes considered effectively timeless. Creatures did not go hungry or age while in the Astral plane. For that reason, its mortal inhabitants needed to return to the Material Plane in order to have children or to reach adulthood.

It was possible to travel to the Astral directly from Wildspace aboard a Spelljammer. In this cosmology, Wildspace was a region where the Prime and the Astral overlapped. Moreover, upon exiting a Wildspace system such as Realmspace, a ship would leave this overlapping region and fully enter the Astral. The border between Wildspace and the Astral Sea had the appearance of a silvery haze.

Once in the Astral, spelljamming pilots could steer their ships in the direction of other Wildspace systems or specific locations in the Astral in the same way that individuals would traverse the plane: by concentrating their thoughts on the destination.

# Eclipsion

In the heart of the void, where stars flicker dimly and forgotten, drifts a shattered world known as Eclipsion. This forsaken, oblong dwarf planet of ancient stone moves aimlessly through the endless darkness. Its surface, etched with shallow valleys and eroded ridges, once might have held meaning, but now it lies featureless, as though time itself has scoured away all memory of its past.

The world is mostly barren—a vast, gray wasteland of lifeless rock, devoid of hope or promise. In its northern hemisphere lay a great depression, shallow yet expansive, the relic of some ancient and unspoken calamity. Long ago, a great sinkhole opened under this area exposing the interior of the planet to the stars. 

Sometime later, some cosmic body collided with Eclipsion, unleashing cataclysmic forces that reshaped the planet. The impact tore away part of the planet, knocking it off its axis and sending it into a slow, ponderous wobble, like a spinning top on the brink of collapse. Explosions of gas and vapor coursed along the surface giving life to two new features: a mass of water that gathered in the depression, now known as the Shallow Sea, and a ghostly atmosphere, thin and fleeting, clinging to the planet like a dying breath.

The collision also revealed a labyrinth of caverns deep beneath the surface—yawning, unknowable, stretching into the very core of Eclipsion. Over the eons, these caves became home to terrors beyond comprehension, things that should not be, things that linger at the edges of Nightmare. Twisted abominations, born from the void, crept from these dark places, and those who ventured too deep into the planet's depths found their dreams infected with horrors that defied reason.

The walls of these caves are marked in places with a strange, hard, and brittle material. When heated, this material transformed into a sticky, tar-like, oozing substance. The exiles who first stumbled upon this forsaken world—outcasts from distant realms—called it Yggdrasil's Sap.

It was a brilliant alchemist among the exiles who first unlocked the secrets of the Sap. Through a method known only to a select few, this substance could be distilled, processed, and shaped into artifacts of immense power—vessels for energy, amplifiers of magic, the fuel for spells of terrifying potency. Following this discovery, the exiles gave their world the name Eclipsion, for it had eclipsed their every dream of success, even as it began corrupting them.

In haste, they built the Undercity—a sprawling, ramshackle collection of crooked streets and half-finished buildings, huddled near the openings of the caves. Then they filled these buildings with any poor, desperate soul that had nowhere else to turn. Miners—colloquially called Sappers—factory laborers, dockhands, builders all toiled endlessly in the oppressive gloom, their faces lined with grime, their spirits as broken as the world they inhabited. 

Further from the mines, stood Midtown, where the former exiles built their own homes. In a stark contrast to the ramshackle slums of the laborers, these grand stone houses, with their ornate facades and intricate wrought iron gates, spoke to the relative luxury their owners now enjoyed. It was a pale imitation of the luxury they might have enjoyed on worlds more fortunate, but to them, it was a world apart from the squalor of the workers below. 

But the exiles would not be alone for long.

From the distant reaches of the Astral Sea came the Noble Githyanki Houses of the U'thar, drawn by rumors of wealth and power. Like vultures, they descended upon Eclipsion, seizing control of the Sap and the planet itself with swift, merciless efficiency. Under the iron fist of the Mi-zrahi family, the Githyanki expanded the operation tenfold, transforming the fractured world into a hub of industry and trade. 

The Mi-zrahi ruled from the Upper City, a gated paradise of wealth and privilege, where sleek towers of metal and glass rose from the shores of the shallow sea. Here, the wonders of both arcane and practical technologies allowed the wealthiest to shape the very land itself—this eerie oasis, vibrant with unnatural life, stood as a mocking testament to the power of wealth, where even the lifeless stone could be bent to serve their desires. 

Crime ran rampant in the lower city, but it was not the Mi-zrahi who truly controlled the streets—it was the Ebon Syndicate. An expansive criminal network, rooted deeply in the underbelly of interstellar society, the Syndicate's reach extends across the known universe, from gambling dens and black markets to manufacturing plants and political offices.

Beneath the guise of a workers' union, the Syndicate held sway over the laborers and sappers of Eclipsion, offering protection and benefits to the downtrodden while keeping them bound by fear and violence. Their reach extended into every corner of the planet’s underbelly, a shadowy web of informants, enforcers, and corrupt officials. The Syndicate’s power was absolute, its influence unseen but ever-present, like the force that once shattered Eclipsion’s surface.

And yet, even here, knowledge had its place. The Celestial Conclave, an ancient order dedicated to the preservation of wisdom, established a university in the shadow of the Upper City. Its soaring gothic towers and sprawling courtyards evoked a sense of gravitas and mystery, a world apart from the chaos that surrounded it. Admission was reserved for the chosen few, almost exclusively from off planet worlds, those whose brilliance was unquestionable. For the rest, the university was a distant dream, a beacon of unreachable light amidst the shadows.

# Shallow Sea

Long before the cataclysmic collision reshaped the planet, the region that would become the Shallow Sea was already marked by geological instability. According to the findings of modern researchers, this area was once a shallow depression, no more than ten meters deep, with narrow trenches similar to those seen across Eclipsion's surface. But at some unknowable point in the past, a colossal sinkhole opened beneath the region, its collapse carving a gaping chasm that plunged hundreds of meters into the planet’s crust, exposing ancient caverns long hidden from the surface.

When the impact flooded the depression with water, the new sea mingled with deposits of Yggdrasil's Sap, sparking volatile reactions beneath the surface. Columns of molten rock erupted from the sea floor, cooling into jagged, blackened spires that now stretch ominously toward the surface. These formations remain active, their surfaces pockmarked with vents that periodically release bursts of energy as the Sap continues its violent interactions deep below.

The chaotic alchemy of Sap and water birthed a new and unnerving evolutionary frontier. Microscopic organisms began to thrive, their forms shaped by the magical essence suffusing the waters. Over time, these organisms gave rise to twisted ecosystems, each more alien than the last. From glowing, jellyfish-like creatures that pulse with eerie light to armored predators with teeth like crystal shards, the lifeforms of the Shallow Sea seem to defy natural law. They are both a testament to adaptation and a harrowing reminder of the chaos unleashed by the Sap’s influence.

Exploring the Shallow Sea is fraught with peril. The waters near the vents are corrosive, the chemical reactions wreaking havoc on both living tissue and machinery. Leviathans, shaped by eons of evolution and the unholy touch of the Sap, lurk in the shadows, patient and ever-hungry, waiting for their next meal to stray too close to the edge of the abyss.

Given these hazards, most ventures into the sea are deemed too unprofitable compared to the relative stability of surface mining. Yet, there are those foolhardy or desperate enough to test their luck, hoping to uncover treasures hidden in the depths.

# Locathah

# **Description:**

The **Locathah** of Eclipsion are a resilient aquatic people who have long adapted to the crushing depths and volatile sap-vent ecology of the undersea world. Their forms are sleek, scale-armored, and adorned with bioluminescent fronds protruding from their mouth, used for communication, ritual, and expression. Few surface dwellers have encountered them directly, and fewer still have earned their trust.

While alien in physiology and custom, Locathah society is complex, deeply spiritual, and tightly bonded through tradition.

---

# **Culture & Society:**

Locathah on Eclipsion live in extended family groups called **broods**, settled around active sap vents deep in the Shallow Sea. These vents provide warmth, energy, and access to sap—a sacred and volatile substance believed to carry the echoes of divine dreams.

Broods range from 20 to 50 individuals and are led by a **Deepmother**—a matriarch chosen for her clarity of vision, connection to the vent’s rhythm, and strength of communal harmony. The Deepmother serves as both spiritual guide and decision-maker for the brood, interpreting the silent pulses of the deep and guiding the brood through visions seen in sap-dreams.

---

# **Mating Habits:**

Locathah mating is a profound and spiritual act, marked by **fusion** and act of great cultural honor and reverence.
* Males are smaller than females and undergo a lifelong preparation for fusion.
* During the **Swaying Dance**, a sacred underwater ritual near the brood’s sap vent, a Deepmother may choose one or more partners.
* Upon selection, the male fuses physically with the female, joining circulatory, hormonal, and spiritual rhythms.
* Fused males become a permanent part of their mate’s body, offering genetic material, immune support, and a deepened spiritual bond as their bodies atrophy.

This fusion is seen as an **act of devotion and legacy**, a noble joining of purpose. To be chosen is considered one of the highest honors in Locathah life—uniting not just body, but will and current, in service to the brood’s future.

---

# **Belief & Spirituality:**

Locathah here believe the sap vents are the **Veins of** The Silent One, an ancient being slumbering beneath the water. They hold that sap is the crystallized residue of the Silent One’s dreams—powerful, dangerous, and sacred. Dreams near vents are prophetic, and mutations from sap exposure are often viewed as divine signs rather than curses.

---

# **Language & Traits:**  

* Speak **Aquan**, layered with rhythmic pulses and frill-flick gestures
* Resistant to sap poisoning common among surface folk
* Master artisans of coral-glass and armor

## **The Hunt:**

**Locathah revere hunting as a sacred rite**. The act of stalking prey in the trenches—amid the shifting currents, bio-luminescent camouflage, and life-threatening terrain—is seen as a **ritual of patience, respect, and unity with the deep**.

Every successful hunt is preceded by a silent moment of communion and followed by a ceremonial offering of bone or scale to the brood’s shrine. The prey is not feared, nor hated, but honored. To hunt well is to **mirror** The Silent One, moving in rhythm with the world’s unseen tides.
